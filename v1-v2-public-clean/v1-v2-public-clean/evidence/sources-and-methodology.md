# Sources, Methodology, and Evidence Labels


## Sources
Prefer original source repositories, pinned commits, verified contract sources, dated documentation, and directly attributable on-chain records. Every claim should link to the exact supporting material rather than only to a summary report.


## Evidence labels
- **Source-confirmed:** behavior or implementation is present in the specified source version.
- **Live-observed:** behavior was observed on a named network or endpoint at a stated date; record method and identifiers.
- **Reproduced:** the behavior was reproduced under documented conditions; provide steps, environment, and result.
- **Conditional:** a potential consequence depends on stated conditions or permissions.
- **Unverified:** evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

These labels describe evidence status, not severity or intent.


## Method
1. Pin the V1 and V2 versions/commits being compared.
2. Identify the component and the exact claim.
3. Link direct evidence and record relevant dates or identifiers.
4. Describe the difference in neutral technical terms.
5. Explain significance separately from observed behavior.
6. State limitations, unanswered questions, and tests not performed.


## Limitations
Public-source review cannot establish that every live deployment uses the inspected source, that private infrastructure matches public documentation, or that a possible vulnerability is exploitable. Unless explicitly documented, dynamic testing, fuzzing, formal verification, and independent peer review have not been performed.


## Archive separation
Private investigative archives may contain original captures, correspondence, notes, drafts, reconstructed documents, or duplicates. Those items are not automatically public evidence. Public claims should be substantiated with sources that can be independently checked and shared responsibly.
